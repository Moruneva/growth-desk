A five-minute review

What to check before handing over digital access

  1. Retrieve the transaction state from your actual merchant account. Export a report if available; do not rely on a checkout screenshot.
  2. Confirm the order identifier, currency and amount. A processor status must be mapped carefully, not guessed.
  3. Take the delivery confirmation from the service that actually grants access, with an independently recorded identifier.
  4. Test the two simplified schemas above using fictional records only. Review each flag as a question for an authorized operator, not as proof.
  5. Keep real merchant and delivery records inside your authorized order system. This prototype stores no audit trail and does not deliver files.

A raw PayPal Activity Download CSV cannot be imported directly. The research schema accepts positive amounts with no more than two decimal places and does not validate currency-specific minor units, refunds, partial deliveries, or multi-item order semantics. Do not transform or paste real financial records into this public trial.

What a “match” actually means

One manually entered row says completed; another says delivered; the IDs, currency and amount agree. This cannot establish that the payment settled, that a file opens, or that the purchaser is entitled to it.

Failure modes worth catching

COMPLETED_NOT_DELIVERED_IN_LOG — paid in the supplied list, no delivery receipt.

DELIVERED_BEFORE_COMPLETION — access appears granted while payment is pending or denied.

REFUND_AFTER_DELIVERY_REVIEW — review whether access remains appropriate after refund.

DUPLICATE_PAYMENT_RECORD / MULTIPLE_DELIVERY_RECORDS — conflicting or repeat rows must be examined.
