Are the right shots actually in the handoff?

Compare two small shot lists—from JSON, CSV or pasted spreadsheet columns: what your edit expects and what the handoff says it contains. See missing scenes, different revisions and technical mismatches before another person tries the files.

No login · No video files · Paste JSON, CSV or spreadsheet rows · Export JSON or CSV reports

What this can verify

This checks recorded metadata only. It cannot open or inspect footage, confirm that a person truly approved a cut, prove rights or find visual continuity mistakes. Treat every clean row as a checklist item for a human to review. Enter fictional or authorized, non-identifying shot codes only; never paste private project text, customer names, filenames, tokens or URLs.

1. Compare two manifests

Each item needs shot_id, revision, fps, width, height and frames. The delivered list also needs review_status: approved, pending or unknown. Use null in JSON or leave spreadsheet cells blank for unknown numbers or frame rate. Paste a JSON array, CSV with a header row, or tab-separated cells copied from a spreadsheet; up to 80 shots per side.

Spreadsheet header (expected): shot_id,revision,fps,width,height,frames. Add ,review_status to the delivered header. Use the same columns separated by tabs when copying cells directly from a spreadsheet. The exported report CSV has different columns and is not a manifest input.

Accepted frame rates: 23.976, 24, 25, 29.97, 30, 50, 59.94, 60 (strings). Shot IDs use up to 24 uppercase letters, digits, hyphens and underscores; duplicates and unrecognised fields are rejected.

3. Decide whether to use the report

If a spreadsheet already solves your handoff, keep using it. This comparison works only on the metadata you enter. Inspect the actual footage and approval records separately. Reports are saved locally when you choose to download them.
